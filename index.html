20 Minutes

The 5 Early Schools of Psychology

Explore how different perspectives emerged to define what psychology should study and how it should study it.

Structuralism

c. 1890s

Key Figure: Edward Titchener & Wilhelm Wundt

Sought to analyze the adult mind in terms of the simplest definable components (sensations, images, feelings) and find how these fit together to form complex experiences.

Method: Controlled Introspection (describing conscious mental structures).

Functionalism

c. 1890s

Key Figure: William James

Influenced by Charles Darwin, it focused on how mental operations and behaviors function to help organisms adapt, survive, and flourish in their environment.

Method: Stream of Consciousness, observation, and psychological testing.

Psychoanalysis

c. 1900s

Key Figure: Sigmund Freud

Emphasized the power of the unconscious mind, early childhood experiences, repressed impulses, and unresolved conflicts in shaping human behavior and personality.

Method: Free association, dream analysis, and clinical case studies.

Behaviorism

c. 1913

Key Figures: John B. Watson & B.F. Skinner

Rejected introspection and the unobservable mind. Argued that psychology should strictly be the objective science of observable behavior conditioned by environmental stimuli.

Method: Laboratory experiments, classical and operant conditioning.

Gestalt Psychology

c. 1912

Key Figures: Max Wertheimer, Koffka & Köhler

Opposed Structuralism, asserting that "The whole is greater than the sum of its parts." Focused on perception and how the human mind organizes chaotic sensory inputs into whole patterns.

Method: Perception experiments, visual problem-solving studies.

Comparative Matrix: Early Schools of Psychology

Examine side-by-side authors, central ideas, and objects of study across all five major historical schools.

Psychological School Key Authors Object of Study Central Ideas & Principles
Structuralism Wilhelm Wundt, Edward Titchener Basic structures of conscious experience Breaks consciousness into basic elements (sensations, emotions) via controlled introspection.
Functionalism William James, John Dewey Functions & purpose of mental processes Examines how mind and behavior enable humans to adapt to environmental demands.
Psychoanalysis Sigmund Freud Unconscious mind & childhood drives Unconscious motives, repressed desires, and early childhood trauma drive human personality.
Behaviorism John B. Watson, B.F. Skinner Observable, measurable behavior Rejects internal unobservable mental states; focuses strictly on stimulus-response associations.
Gestalt Max Wertheimer, Wolfgang Köhler Perceptual organization as a whole "The whole is greater than the sum of its parts." Mind organizes perception into meaningful patterns.
